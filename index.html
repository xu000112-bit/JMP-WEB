Job Market Paper
Soft Hospital Resilience Under Stress: Evidence from Healthcare-Associated Infections During COVID-19
Qinzheng Xu

This paper studies when communication-related soft resilience becomes more consequential for infection control performance. I use pre-pandemic hospital communication quality as a proxy for this capacity. The analysis combines HCAHPS, CMS Hospital Compare, and Hospital Cost Report data for 3,366 U.S. hospitals from 2018 to 2021. I use the onset of COVID-19 as a common external stress shock. The empirical design examines whether infection outcomes changed differently after COVID-19 across hospitals with different pre-pandemic communication quality. The main relationship appears in BSI outcomes with higher COVID-induced diagnostic noise. A one-point increase in pre-pandemic communication quality is associated with about a 10 percent smaller post-COVID change in these infection rates relative to their sample means. No comparable pattern appears for non-BSI outcomes with lower COVID-induced diagnostic noise. I then examine two additional margins of effective task-related noise. The relationship is stronger among hospitals with less prior BSI-related experience. It also becomes stronger as local COVID-19 intensity rises for BSI outcomes with higher COVID-induced diagnostic noise. The results are robust to hospital fixed effects, pre-pandemic medical delivery quality, staffing, alternative communication measures, and sample restrictions. Together, the findings show that communication-related soft resilience is more strongly associated with infection-control performance when effective task-related noise is greater.
